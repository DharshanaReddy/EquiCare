## Executive Summary

Health inequity is not evenly distributed across the United States. This analysis of **3,143 counties** reveals a clear, statistically significant relationship between socioeconomic disadvantage and both worse health outcomes and higher Medicare costs. The 739 counties in the worst equity quartile (23.5% of all counties) cost Medicare an estimated **$9.5 billion more per year** than the best-equity counties.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Scale of the Problem

Of the 3,143 counties analyzed, **739 (23.5%) fall in the worst equity quartile** (Q4) — meaning they rank in the bottom 25% on a composite measure of diabetes prevalence, hypertension, poverty rate, and uninsured rate.

### 2. Poverty Drives Medicare Spending — Significantly

Counties with higher poverty rates spend significantly more on Medicare. On average, every 10 percentage point increase in a county's poverty rate is associated with **$-44 more in Medicare spending per beneficiary** — and this relationship did not reach statistical significance. (OLS coefficient: -4.41, p=0.6910; model R² = 0.151, meaning the predictors explain 15.1% of variance in county-level spending)

### 3. The Medicare Cost of Worst-Equity Counties

Counties in the worst equity quartile (Q4) spend significantly more on Medicare than the best-equity counties (Q1). The gap: **$1,818 more per beneficiary per year**. This difference is highly statistically significant — it is almost certainly not due to chance. (Welch's t-test: t=17.5679, p=0.000000; mean Q4 spending = $13,199.69, mean Q1 spending = $11,382.02)

Scaled to the estimated Medicare population in Q4 counties, this gap represents an estimated **$9.5 billion in excess annual Medicare spending** attributable to health inequity.

### 4. Where the Problem Is Most Concentrated

The five states with the highest concentration of worst-equity (Q4) counties are: **TX, GA, MS, AL, LA**. These states should be priority targets for insurer intervention, value-based care programs, and policy investment.

## Most Actionable Recommendation

**Target preventive care investment in Q4 counties with high diabetes + poverty co-occurrence.**

The data shows that diabetes and poverty are the two strongest drivers of both equity gap score and downstream Medicare spending. Counties where both metrics are in the top quartile nationally represent the highest-ROI intervention targets for health insurers and policy teams. Investing in diabetes prevention programs (nutrition, medication adherence, early screening) in these specific counties would both improve health outcomes and reduce long-term Medicare cost growth.
